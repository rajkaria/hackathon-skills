# Stage Presence Cheat Sheet

Delivery mechanics for live pitches. Everything below is the difference between a 7/10 and a 9/10 from the same script.

**Use when:** Preparing for any live pitch ≥ 60 seconds with an audience.
**Skip if:** Async submission only (video-only tracks).

---

## Body — 6 rules

1. **Feet planted.** Shoulder-width, toes forward. No rocking, no pacing. Lock in for the first 30s of the pitch.
2. **Hands in the 'box'** between navel and sternum. Gesture inside this box — outside feels chaotic, below feels defeated.
3. **One anchor per beat.** When you hit the problem, point at the screen. When you hit the moat, step forward once. One move per narrative beat, not continuous motion.
4. **Eye contact rotation.** 3 seconds per judge. Rotate deliberately. If there are 5 judges, the full sweep takes 15s — that's the length of one beat.
5. **No podium.** Step out from behind it if possible. Podiums eat energy and frame you as a presenter, not a founder.
6. **Stop moving for the close.** Last 5 seconds: feet still, hands down, eyes on the lead judge. Land the line. Silence.

---

## Voice — 5 rules

1. **Open low.** First sentence 1 half-step lower than your normal voice. Signals authority; you can climb from there.
2. **Slow the hook.** First 8 words at 70% normal speed. Let them register.
3. **Pause before the number.** "We have... [0.5s]... 340 signed receipts." The pause makes the number remembered.
4. **Drop volume for emphasis, don't raise it.** Shouting = nerves. A quiet sentence in a loud room pulls attention.
5. **Kill your fillers.** Record yourself. Count every "um / like / basically / so yeah / you know." Every one ≥ 3 times in 3 minutes is a tell. Replace with a closed-mouth pause.

---

## Slide design — 8 rules

1. **One idea per slide.** If you can't describe the slide in 7 words, split it.
2. **No bullet points.** Replace with: one headline, one image, one number. Bullets make judges read instead of listen.
3. **36pt minimum.** If it's < 36pt, the back row can't read it. Delete it.
4. **Dark background, high-contrast accent.** Slate-950 background with one accent color. Matches the landing page matches the OG image matches the demo.
5. **No stock photos.** Ever. Use your actual product screens or a single illustrated icon.
6. **No logos grid.** If you must show ecosystem logos, show 3 max, at equal size, on one line.
7. **Slide transitions off.** No fades, no spins. Cut hard between slides. Makes the pitch feel decisive.
8. **Blackout slides between beats.** A fully-black slide for 2 seconds between the problem and the demo forces the audience to look at you. Use 2-3 per pitch.

---

## Prop usage

**When props work:**
- A phone mirrored to screen showing a live action (mobile demo).
- A physical artifact the product generates (printed receipt, NFC card).
- A single object that makes the abstract concrete (a $1 bill when talking about micropayments).

**When props fail:**
- Dongles, adapters, cable juggling. Test the A/V 60 minutes before your slot.
- Laptop passed between teammates. Designate one driver.
- Anything that requires you to stop talking to fiddle with it.

**Rule:** If the prop requires you to turn your back or break eye contact for > 3 seconds, cut it.

---

## Broken demo — the recovery script

This happens. Have the recovery rehearsed.

### Layer 1 — Pre-recorded backup (required)
- 20-second MP4 of the demo, muted, autoplaying on loop.
- Stored locally on the presenting laptop AND on a USB.
- Linked from Slide 1 of the deck as a hidden fallback.

### Layer 2 — If the live demo stalls mid-pitch:
```
1. Do not apologize. "Apologies, it's not loading" = 2 seconds of lost credibility.
2. Say one neutral sentence: "Let me cut to the backup — the mechanics are the same."
3. Trigger the MP4. Keep talking over it as if it were live.
4. Do not return to the live demo even if it recovers mid-video.
5. In Q&A: "The live service is up at [URL], happy to demo 1-on-1 after."
```

### Layer 3 — If the whole laptop dies:
- Have the pitch deck printed as A4 handouts (5 copies).
- Pitch from the handout. Judges will remember the composure more than the missing demo.
- You score lower than a clean demo, but higher than the team that panicked.

### The line that saves it:
> "The internet is optional; the idea is not. Let me walk you through what the product does."

Used verbatim after a 2024 Bench demo crash. Net score impact: estimated -5% vs a clean demo; estimated +15% vs a visible meltdown.

## Energy management (the hours before)

**T-2 hours:**
- Eat a real meal, not a sponsor bar. Low blood sugar kills delivery.
- Drink water, not coffee after T-4h. Coffee tremors are visible from the third row.

**T-60 min:**
- Do the full pitch once, standing, out loud. Not in your head.
- Test A/V in the actual room if allowed.

**T-15 min:**
- Three slow nasal breaths, 6s in 6s out. Drops heart rate by 8-12 bpm.
- Do NOT review notes. You're done rehearsing.

**T-0:**
- Walk up. Plant feet. Lower first sentence half a step. Go.

## Post-pitch (the 90 seconds nobody optimizes)

When you land the close and judges start applauding:
1. Nod once, don't bow.
2. Stay on stage until explicitly dismissed — do not bolt.
3. When you step off, walk **toward** the judge table, not the crowd.
4. If a judge makes eye contact, nod. Do not initiate conversation unless invited.
5. In the hallway, the first 5 people who ask questions are often judges' assistants scoring peripheral signals. Treat them identically to judges.

---

## Team orchestration (2-3 presenter setups)

**Default:** one presenter, one driver, one silent.
- Presenter: eyes on judges, never on laptop.
- Driver: eyes on laptop, never on judges. Advances slides, handles demo.
- Silent: on side of stage, available for targeted Q&A ("the security question — [Name] led that, want to grab this one?").

**Never:** three presenters alternating. Looks uncoordinated. Judges lose track of who they're scoring.

**Handoffs:** If you must hand off, do it with a full sentence, not a glance.
> "I'll pass it to [Name] who led the Stellar integration."
Hand off, step back, go silent. Let them own the next 60 seconds fully.

---

## Rehearsal minimum

- **Solo, mirror:** 5 full passes.
- **Solo, recorded:** 3 passes. Watch back at 1× and 1.5×.
- **Team, standing, timed:** 2 passes.
- **Team, with a friendly non-team human watching:** 1 pass.

**Total:** 11 passes. Most teams do 2. The 9-pass gap is one of the largest in the whole skill.
