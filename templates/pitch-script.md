# Pitch Script — 5 Minute Stage Version

> Spoken word: ~700 words at presenter pace. Practice with a timer. If your draft is >800 words, cut. If it's <600, you're under-using the time.

> Use this template alongside `templates/build-spec.md` (where the narrative arc was defined). The four shorter variants (15s / 60s / 3min / 30min) live in `phases/pitch-variants.md` (Sprint 2).

---

## [0:00 - 0:15] HOOK

> Open with a specific person and a specific weekly pain. No "imagine if..." No "in today's world..." Lead with a name.

**[VISUAL: full-screen photo or illustration of the persona]**

"This is Sarah. She runs a 15-person DAO. Every Sunday she spends three hours sending crypto payments to her contributors — copying addresses, signing transactions, manually tracking who's been paid. Three hours. Every week. For 156 hours a year, Sarah is doing what a script could do in three seconds."

---

## [0:15 - 0:45] PROBLEM

> Make judges feel the pain. Quantify. Use plain language.

**[VISUAL: screenshot of Sarah's actual workflow — spreadsheet + wallet + Gnosis Safe + Etherscan tabs]**

"There are 12,000 DAOs with treasuries over $100k. None of them have native payroll. They're using Gnosis Safe for security, a spreadsheet for tracking, and a person — usually the most senior contributor — manually executing payments. It's the most expensive task in the org being done by the most expensive person. And it doesn't have to be."

---

## [0:45 - 1:15] SOLUTION

> Your one-liner. Then show the product.

**[VISUAL: product screen — the dashboard]**

"HashPay automates crypto payroll in one click. You connect your DAO's treasury, you upload a CSV of contributors with their addresses and amounts, and HashPay handles the rest. Recurring payments. Multi-signature approvals. On-chain receipts. Sarah's three hours becomes thirty seconds."

---

## [1:15 - 3:00] DEMO

> Live or pre-recorded walkthrough. Show the product working. No architecture diagrams.

**[VISUAL: screen recording of the happy path, auto-playing]**

[NARRATE OVER THE VIDEO — every action you describe should match what's on screen at that moment.]

"Sarah connects her treasury wallet... she uploads her contributor list... she sets the cadence — every Sunday, automatic... she funds the payroll once... and from now on, every Sunday morning at 9 AM, fifteen contributors get paid, on-chain, with verifiable receipts. Here's the latest run from this morning — fifteen transactions, all settled, all verifiable on Etherscan."

## [3:00 - 3:30] IMPACT

> Who benefits, how many, what's the market.

"There are 12,000 DAOs spending an average of $25k/year on payroll operations. That's a $300M annual market just in DAO payroll — before we expand to crypto-native companies, freelance collectives, and contributor networks. We charge 0.1% per payment. The math works."

## Q&A Prep (Not Spoken — Memorize)

For the 12 questions every judge asks, see `phases/q-and-a-combat.md` (Sprint 2). The five most likely for THIS pitch:

1. **"Why hasn't [bigger company] done this?"** → "[Bigger company] is solving treasury, not payroll. Different problem, different buyer."
2. **"What's the moat?"** → "Recurring payment graph + contributor identity layer. Stickier than competitors who do one-off."
3. **"What's mocked?"** → "Mainnet integration is testnet for the hackathon. Everything else is live."
4. **"How do you reach the first 100 customers?"** → "I've been in 8 DAO operator Telegrams for 18 months. I have a list of 47 ops leads who've already complained about this."
5. **"What's your biggest risk?"** → "Custody UX. We've designed around it but it's the question we'll keep refining."

## Delivery Discipline

- **Speak slower than feels natural.** Most pitchers rush — drops perceived confidence.
- **Pause after key lines.** Let the hook land. Let the vision land.
- **Eye contact with one judge per section.** Rotate.
- **Don't apologize.** No "we ran out of time for..." No "this isn't perfect but..." Run the show smoothly.
- **End on the line, not on "thank you."** The thank you is reflex; the line is what they remember.
